Formal models of reasoning under cognitive constraints.

email ↗ Stanford profile ↗ publications ↓

I am a Postdoctoral Fellow at the Technical University of Denmark. I do research on formal models of reasoning and rationality in multi-agent and epistemic settings. My work has been primarily in logic, focused on various forms of belief and belief formation under cognitive constraints such as unawareness and inattention. Recently, I became very interested in implicit bias and implicit attitudes, where I am working to apply formal modeling to get a clearer view on some fundamental concepts in the empirical research. An illustrative example here is the relation between associative and propositional thinking: how exactly are these two related? How is propositional reasoning affected by the associations we make? And vice versa, is associative reasoning ever impacted by propositional thinking, and if so, how? These questions are also very interesting from the perspective of logic, typically based on propositional accounts of reasoning.

I earned my PhD at the University of Copenhagen, with a dissertation on logical models of reasoning that mainly explores how this is affected by explicit and implicit cognitive phenomena, such as attention, awareness, and implicit inferences.

Beyond questions of rationality, I am fascinated by methodological issues such as: "why are mathematical models so illuminating about reality, when they abstract away so many details?"
